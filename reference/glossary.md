# Glossary

[← Home](../README.md) · [Techniques](techniques.md)

Boatbuilding vocabulary, in plain terms — mostly words I had to look up myself.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Amine blush** | Waxy film that forms on curing epoxy. Wash it off before sanding or recoating. |
| **Batten** | Long, flexible strip used to draw or check a fair curve. |
| **Bulkhead** | Transverse structural panel. Often doubles as the shape-defining station mold. |
| **Chine** | The line where the bottom meets the side. |
| **Chine log** | Timber strip along the chine that the bottom and side panels land on. |
| **Fair** | A curve with no bumps or flats. "Fairing" is the work of getting there. |
| **Fillet** | Rounded bead of thickened epoxy in an inside corner, spreading the load. |
| **Fore / aft** | Toward the bow / toward the stern. |
| **Frame** | Transverse structural member. |
| **Garboard** | The plank next to the keel. |
| **Gunwale** | The rail along the top edge of the hull side. Pronounced "gunnel". |
| **Limber hole** | Notch letting bilge water drain to the low point. |
| **Lofting** | Drawing the boat's lines full size to get the shapes off the plans. |
| **Rocker** | Fore-and-aft curve of the bottom. |
| **Scarf** | Long tapered joint used to join plywood or timber end to end. |
| **Sheathing** | Fibreglass cloth and epoxy over plywood, for abrasion and water resistance. |
| **Sheer** | The top edge of the hull side, and its curve seen from the side. |
| **Station** | A numbered position along the length; where a bulkhead or mold sits. |
| **Stringer** | Fore-and-aft stiffener on the inside of the planking. |
| **Strongback / build frame** | The rigid base the boat is built on. |
| **Transom** | The flat panel at the stern. |
| **Wet out** | Saturating wood or glass with unthickened epoxy until it goes clear. |
